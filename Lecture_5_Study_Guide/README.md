# Week 5: Teamwork and Team Dynamics - Study Guide

## 1. Introduction to Teams

**What is a Team?**
- A **team** is a group of people who are **interdependent** regarding information, resources, knowledge, and skills.
- They aim to **combine their efforts** to achieve a **common goal**.

| Feature | Working Group | Team |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Performance Focus** | Based on what members do as **individuals**. | Includes both **individual results** and **collective work products**. |

**Types of Teams:**
- **Large Teams:** 20-30 members (e.g., IT Department Lecturers, Senate)
- **Small Teams:** 2-5 members (e.g., Board of Directors, Surgeons in surgery)
- **Short Term:** Temporary teams for specific events (e.g., Event organizing committee)
- **Long Term:** Permanent or ongoing teams (e.g., Parliament standing committee for Public Relations)

> **Real-World Example:** In a hospital setting, a group of doctors eating lunch in the cafeteria is simply a **working group**. However, surgeons, nurses, and anesthetists performing a surgery together form a **team** because their success depends interdependently on each other to achieve the **common goal** of saving the patient.

## 2. Team Development Stages (Dr. Bruce Tuckman)
Dr. Bruce Tuckman (1965) identified **five stages** of group and team development:

| Stage | Description | Member Behavior |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Forming** | The team acquaints and establishes **ground rules**. | Formalities are preserved; members treat each other as strangers. |
| **2. Storming** | Members start communicating feelings but view themselves individually. | Resistance to control by leaders; potential show of hostility. |
| **3. Norming** | People feel part of the team and realize work can be completed. | Members accept other viewpoints and start collaborating. |
| **4. Performing** | The team works in an **open and trusting atmosphere**. | Flexibility is key, and strict hierarchy is of little importance. |
| **5. Adjourning** | The team conducts an assessment of the work done. | Planning transitions and recognizing members' contributions before disbanding. |

> **Real-World Example:** When a group of students is assigned a university project, they first awkwardly introduce themselves (**Forming**). Then they may argue about who will do which part of the work (**Storming**). Once they agree on roles and rules, they start cooperating (**Norming**). They work smoothly together to finish the project (**Performing**), and finally present it and celebrate their success before splitting up (**Adjourning**).

## 3. Team Roles and Belbin's Theory

**What is a Role?**
- A **role** is a set of behaviors required or expected of a person occupying a certain position within a group.
- **Formal Role:** Dictated by the organizational structure (e.g., Team Captain, Coach).
- **Informal Role:** Evolves naturally from the group's dynamics.
- **Role Acceptance** is crucial to enhance a group's structure and performance.

### Belbin's 9 Team Roles
Dr. R. Meredith Belbin defined a team role as: *"A tendency to behave, contribute, and interrelate with others in a particular way."* He identified **9 Team Roles**, grouped into **three main categories**:

| Category | Role | Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Action-Oriented** | **Shaper** | Sees obstacles as challenges, pushes things, extroverted, stimulates others. |
| | **Implementer** | Disciplined, efficient, puts concepts into practical actions. (Can be inflexible). |
| | **Completer Finisher** | Perfectionist, works systematically to finish. (May be reluctant to delegate). |
| **People-Oriented** | **Co-ordinator** | Supportive, caring to people, good negotiator, flexible. |
| | **Teamworker** | Diplomatic and popular. (Can be poor at making difficult decisions). |
| | **Resource Investigator** | Extroverted, overly optimistic. (May lose enthusiasm quickly). |
| **Thought-Oriented** | **Plant** | Innovator, brings new concepts/ideas, introverted. (Impractical, poor communicator). |
| | **Monitor Evaluator** | Critical thinker, carefully weighs pros and cons, unemotional to others. |
| | **Specialist** | Single-minded, professional, highly knowledgeable in a specific area. |

> **Real-World Example:** In a software development team, the **Plant** designs a creative new app concept. The **Monitor Evaluator** analyzes its feasibility. The **Implementer** writes the actual code, while the **Completer Finisher** thoroughly tests it to ensure there are absolutely zero bugs before the release.

## 4. Problems with Roles and Solutions

When working in teams, individuals can face multiple challenges related to their roles:

| Problem | Description | Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Role Overload** | Attempting to get too much done in less time. | Exhaustion, stress builds, productivity falls, possible isolation. |
| **Role Conflict** | Group members have different expectations of someone's responsibilities, or two roles clash. | Person feels torn or faces a moral/ethical dilemma. |
| **Role Ambiguity** | A role is left too vague or undefined. | Self-confidence drops, severe drop in efficiency, isolation. |
| **Rigidity** | Sticking rigidly to a specific role. | Prevents flexibility and exploring other hidden potentials. |
| **Abdication** | Relinquishing the duties of a role or refusing to help others because "it's not my responsibility." | Creates a damaging gap in teamwork. |
| **Territoriality** | Reluctance to let others contribute to one's own assigned role; guarding personal space. | Limits collaboration and halts team growth. |
| **Human Nature** | The habit of being unpredictable or inconsistent within the same role. | Strays from expected behavior and confuses the team. |

**Handling Role Problems:**
1. Have **solid, unemotional discussions**.
2. Practice **role re-delegation** until responsibilities have settled comfortably on the correct person.
3. Utilize **self-evaluation** and **personality testing** to ensure correct role placement.

**Alternative to the Role Concept:**
- **Empowering** the team members.
- Focusing on **multiskilling** people so they become **all-rounders** rather than being stuck in one rigid role.

> **Real-World Example:** A politician holding a high government position while simultaneously acting as the Chairman of an opposing political party could experience severe **inter-role conflict** and **role ambiguity**, as the two roles demand entirely conflicting loyalties and actions.

## 5. Teamwork and Group Dysfunctions

**What is Teamwork?**
- Teamwork is the **cooperation** between members with diverse skills and backgrounds to achieve a common goal.
- It is the energy that keeps a team together. Without teamwork, individual players cannot perform better collectively.

**Characteristics of a Successful Team:**
- Works together toward **common goals**.
- Perceives the relevance of the group's tasks.
- Maintains **mutual respect** for all members.
- Embraces a **diversity** of member skills and experiences.
- Respects **individual communication styles**.

**The Dysfunctions of Groups and Teams:**
- **Groupthink:** A dangerous psychological phenomenon where group members prioritize consensus and avoiding conflict over making the correct decision. This leads to a lack of critical thinking and poor choices.

> **Real-World Example:** A successful tech startup has developers, marketers, and designers (**diverse skills**) working together efficiently to launch a product (**common goal**). Because they maintain **mutual respect**, they avoid **Groupthink** by encouraging everyone to safely voice their critical opinions or suggest improvements.
